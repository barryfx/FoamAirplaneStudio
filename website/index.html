A drawing-first workflow

From plans to parts.

Keep your reference drawing in view while you shape the airplane, fit the assembly, and prepare individual parts for export.

  1. 01

    Bring your plans

    Load PNG, JPG or multipage PDF drawings. Set the wingspan or use the reference image scale.

  2. 02

    Shape the model

    Trace outlines, assign airfoils and profiles, and generate the wing, fuselage and stabilizers.

  3. 03

    Fit & export

    Position components, cut mating seats, check weight and balance, then export your parts.

Made for foam aircraft

The details that make
a model your own.

01 / WING

Build around your airfoil

Import or sketch airfoils, smooth traced profiles, and export DAT files. Add panel dihedral, spars, control surfaces, hinge relief and lightening pockets.

02 / FUSELAGE

Shape it from the inside out

Edit cross-section profiles and wall thickness. Generate separate mirrored halves with alignment pins, fitted formers, a servo tray and mirrored carbon stiffener grooves. Choose open or closed ends.

03 / CUTS & TAIL

Make room for the hardware

Cut any of four fuselage surfaces and retain cut-outs as separate parts. Design horizontal and vertical stabilizers with elevator and rudder hinge bevels.

04 / ASSEMBLY

See how everything fits

Position and rotate parts in 0.5-degree increments. Make reversible mating cuts, inspect individual components, and give exported parts meaningful names.

05 / WEIGHT & BALANCE

Plan the balance

Place RC components on the side view. Combine their weight with foam, plywood, carbon-fiber spars/stiffeners and fiberglass cloth/resin to calculate total weight, CG and wing loading.

06 / YOUR WORKFLOW

Keep designing

Undo and redo edits, reuse cached models, and cancel background generation. Portable project files keep your reference drawings and design inputs together.

Included with every installer

Start with the
Baby Buzzard.

The BabyBuzzard36.foam example and its reference PDF are included, so you can explore a project before starting your own.

On Windows, look in Example inside the installation folder. On Linux, look in /usr/share/foamairplanestudio/Example.

Open the project with File → Open, then use Save As to keep your own editable copy.
